About WILDE

Western Himalayan Initiative for Landscapes, Diversities and Ecologies

For too long, researchers, local communities, farmers, educators, and practitioners in the Western Himalayas have operated in silos—reinventing the wheel, facing parallel struggles, and lacking a unified bridge to share knowledge. When a farmer seeks answers about shifting weather patterns, or when a researcher looks for on-the-ground collaborators outside academia, the pathways are often missing.

WILDE is a collaborative alliance designed to shatter these barriers. We bring together researchers, local stakeholders, students, and grassroots practitioners into a single multi-sector dialogue. Our mission is to co-create sustainable solutions for conservation, agriculture, and livelihood challenges across the Western Himalayan landscape by weaving together traditional wisdom, community lived experiences, and modern scientific research.

Conference aim & goals

What this year’s conference sets out to do

Aim

To empower the next generation of students and researchers with versatile, interdisciplinary tools and methodologies, fostering a culture of collaborative problem-solving, open science, and holistic landscape stewardship in the Western Himalayas.

Goal 1

Bridge academic silos

Expose college students from diverse backgrounds (sciences, arts, humanities, and commerce) to cross-disciplinary methodologies like GIS, statistical modeling, science communication, and digital tools.

Goal 2

Promote practical skill-building

Provide hands-on training through workshops (e.g., QGIS, R-stats, zine-making, and AI literacy) that are broadly applicable across multiple career paths.

Goal 3

Connect research to reality

Showcase diverse research talks focused on Western Himalayan or Himalayan landscapes so students can see real-world applications of analytical tools in regional conservation and development.

Goal 4

Cultivate a regional network

Build an active, supportive network of young scholars and practitioners dedicated to collaborative, community-connected research in Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Jammu & Kashmir, and Ladakh.

Competition rules at a glance

  • Subject: Ecology, conservation, or society–environment relationships. Draw on published research, your own work, field observations, or documented environmental issues.
  • Format: An article for a general audience — explainer, research feature, narrative non-fiction, evidence-based opinion, or creative pieces that communicate research.
  • Length: 800–1,000 words (title + article text; references and bio excluded).
  • Include: Title, short author bio (up to ~50–75 words), and references or source links. Submit as PDF or DOCX.
  • Originality: Original work only; acknowledge all sources. Disclose any generative AI use.
  • Judging (100 points): Accuracy & evidence (25), subject understanding (20), accessibility (20), clarity (20), originality & engagement (15).

Submit to saryanfoundation@gmail.com by 5 November 2026. Suggested file name: YourName_ArticleTitle_Language.docx

Science and Community Expo Zone

Setting up an interactive presence in our Science and Community Expo Zone is a wonderful way to connect directly with local students who rarely get access to national-level institutional networks.

Share this section: #expo

Instead of traditional, static booths, we invite you to design your space in whatever format best fits your work. Here are a few ideas you can explore:

Idea 1

AMA Mentorship Stations

Host informal sessions where your team can discuss career paths, graduate opportunities, and research internships with local students at your organisation.

Idea 2

Hands-On Science and Field Gear Demos

Bring along field equipment (such as camera traps, acoustic monitors, or sampling tools) to let students get hands-on experience with field methodologies and ecological research.

Idea 3

Eco-Merchandise and Resource Corner

Share educational materials, field guides, or showcase sustainable crafts, regional produce, and artisan takeaways (including community or Self-Help Group initiatives).

The Science and Community Expo will have designated slots on both days of the WILDE conference. In addition to dedicated time slots, you can set up and interact with participants whenever it best fits your team’s schedule during the event.

Guidelines

Guidelines for submission

Conference theme

Interdisciplinary Research for Himalayan Landscapes, Ecologies, and Communities

Themes for talks and posters

  1. Theme 1: Himalayan Ecology & Biodiversity — Flora, fauna, high-altitude species, insect diversity
  2. Theme 2: Climate Change & Landscape Dynamics — Glacial retreat, treeline shifts, forest ecosystems
  3. Theme 3: Art, Illustration & Creative Science Communication — Eco-zines, drawing, photography, creative storytelling for community outreach
  4. Theme 4: Community Conservation & Traditional Knowledge — Human-wildlife coexistence, local stewardship
  5. Theme 5: AI, Data Science & Tech in Ecology — Machine learning, camera traps, bioacoustics, bio-GIS
  6. Open Track / Other Research — If your research does not fit the themes above but is relevant to the ecology of the Himalayan landscape, choose this.

Talk guidelines

  • Presenters in open sessions will have 12 minutes to present their work, followed by 5 minutes for discussion and Q&A.
  • Presenters will receive their presentation time slots and related details by email.
  • Talk selection decisions and the detailed schedule, including presentation dates and time slots, will be shared by 5 November 2026.

Poster guidelines

  • Posters may be up to a maximum size of 4 ft (width) × 3 ft (height).
  • Presenters may print their posters on any material of their choice.
  • All printing costs must be borne by the presenter. Printing facilities will not be available at the venue.
